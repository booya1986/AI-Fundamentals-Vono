# Meeting Formats

Vono runs four standard meeting types. Don't invent new ones.

## 1. Kickoff (week 1)

**Duration**: 90 minutes
**Attendees**: All sponsor-side stakeholders, Vono lead, Vono key team
**Goal**: Align on the one-pager + commit to ways of working

### Agenda (90 min)

1. **Introductions** (10 min) - around the room, name + role + what success looks like
2. **One-pager walkthrough** (20 min) - Vono walks through; client confirms or refines
3. **Project plan + milestones** (15 min) - phase dates, key gates, when we meet
4. **Roles & decision rights** (15 min) - RACI, escalation path, decision-authority thresholds
5. **Risks** (15 min) - top 3 risks each side sees, mitigation owners
6. **Ways of working** (10 min) - meeting cadence, communication tools, response times
7. **Action items + close** (5 min) - commitments before next meeting

### Pre-work

Send to all attendees 48 hours before:
- The signed one-pager
- The proposed project plan (high-level)
- The proposed RACI

People should arrive having read these.

### Output

Within 24 hours after the kickoff:
- Meeting summary in Vono format
- Updated one-pager (if anything changed)
- First weekly status report scheduled

## 2. Weekly checkpoint (with sponsor)

**Duration**: 30 minutes
**Frequency**: Same day same time, every week
**Attendees**: Vono lead + client sponsor (only)
**Goal**: Surface risks early, get decisions, maintain trust

### Agenda (30 min)

1. **RAG + headline** (3 min) - "We're amber. Reason: vendor X is missing two key clauses."
2. **What changed this week** (5 min) - status update
3. **Decisions needed** (10 min) - 1-3 decisions, with options
4. **Risks** (5 min) - what's emerging that the sponsor should know
5. **What's next** (5 min) - what to expect next week
6. **Sponsor questions** (2 min)

### No-go: making the sponsor read

The sponsor's job in this meeting is to listen, decide, and connect. Don't make them read documents during the meeting. If they need to read, send 24 hours in advance.

### Output

- Decisions captured in writing within 4 hours
- Updated risk register
- Status report sent on Tuesday (after Tuesday's checkpoint)

---

## 3. Bi-weekly steering (with executive)

**Duration**: 30 minutes
**Frequency**: Every 2 weeks
**Attendees**: Vono lead, client sponsor, client business owner, executive sponsor (CIO/CFO/CEO)
**Goal**: Strategic alignment, escalation handling, executive visibility

### Agenda (30 min)

1. **Slide 1: status** (3 min) - RAG + 3-line summary
2. **Slide 2: decisions needed today** (15 min) - 1-2 strategic decisions
3. **Slides 3-N: context** (5 min) - if needed
4. **Slide N+1: next milestone** (3 min)
5. **Executive questions** (4 min)

### Critical rule

The executive should never learn bad news in this meeting. By the time it's in steering, the sponsor has heard it in the prior weekly. The steering meeting is for confirming and committing, not surprising.

If you must bring bad news, **brief the sponsor first**. Walk in aligned.

### Pre-work

Send the deck 24 hours in advance. The executive may have read it; they may not have. Either way, walk through it briefly.

### Output

- Decisions captured
- Action items with named owners
- Updated project plan (if scope changed)
- Brief recap email to attendees within 4 hours

## 4. Phase-gate review

**Duration**: 30-60 minutes
**Frequency**: At the end of each phase
**Attendees**: Vono lead, sponsor, business owner, tech lead (if relevant)
**Goal**: Formal sign-off on the prior phase + go/no-go for next phase

### Agenda

1. **Phase recap** (10 min) - what was the goal, what we did, what we found
2. **Deliverable walkthrough** (15-30 min) - the document/artifact produced
3. **Recommendations for next phase** (5 min) - approach, risks, dependencies
4. **Decision** (5 min) - go / no-go / conditional go
5. **Open items** (5 min) - what carries over

### Sign-off

Sign-off must be in writing. Either:
- Signed page in the deliverable document
- Email confirmation: "Approved. Proceed to phase X."
- Documented decision in the project log

Verbal "yeah, sounds good" is not sign-off. If you proceed without written sign-off and something goes wrong, you have no defense.

### When the answer is "no-go"

Sometimes the phase reveals that the original plan won't work. That's fine. Steps:
1. Vono lead and sponsor pre-meet to align on the path forward.
2. Phase-gate meeting includes the new path as a recommendation, not a surprise.
3. The path forward is documented as a phase deliverable.
4. If significant scope/budget change - update the SOW.

## Optional meetings (use sparingly)

### Working session
**When**: A specific deliverable needs collaborative work.
**Duration**: 90-120 min.
**Format**: Whiteboard + screen-share. Decisions captured live.

### Stakeholder interview
**When**: Discovery phase, gathering input from a specific stakeholder.
**Duration**: 30-60 min.
**Format**: Open-ended questions, take notes, summarize after.

### Pilot review
**When**: After pilot deployment, before wider rollout.
**Duration**: 60 min.
**Format**: Pilot users + business owner. What worked, what didn't, what needs to change.

## Anti-patterns

### Meeting without an agenda
If you can't write the agenda, you don't know what the meeting is for. Cancel.

### "Status meeting"
Pure status meetings (no decisions) are inefficient. Status goes in writing. Meetings are for decisions.

### 60+ minute meetings as default
Most decisions can be made in 30 min. If you need 60 min, ask why.

### Recurring meetings that never die
At every quarter, ask: "Is this meeting still useful?" If not, kill it.

### Decision deferred without owner
If a decision is deferred to "next meeting", that's a problem. Either commit a decider or document explicitly who'll decide and when.

### Side conversations that affect the project
A 5-minute hallway chat that changes a decision is dangerous. Always: "Let me capture that and send a quick note." Then send the note.

## Tools

Vono house default:
- **Calendar**: client's preferred (Outlook / Google Calendar)
- **Documents**: shared drive (client's choice)
- **Status updates**: email + persistent doc in shared drive
- **Action tracking**: a single shared sheet or task tracker

Avoid: too many tools. One pane of glass for the project.
